## The three misses

| Outcome | Venue, coin | Expiry (UTC) | Threshold | hyperkeeper's TWAP | Margin | hyperkeeper said | Venue settled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10514 | `skew` BTC | Oct 10 00:00 | 82,583 | 82,578.51 | −0.54 bp | NO | YES |
| 10877 | `out` ETH | Oct 10 21:30 | 2,505.8 | 2,505.798 | −0.01 bp | NO | YES |
| 11198 | `skew` BTC | Oct 11 18:00 | 83,803 | 83,825.34 | +2.67 bp | YES | NO |

- **10877 is a tie.** 0.01 bp is a fifth of a cent on ETH. hyperkeeper samples the mark price from the WebSocket, and the venue reads it on its own schedule, so at this distance either answer is possible.
- **11198 points at a different average.** hyperkeeper weights each trade price by how long it stood. In this window BTC fell from about 83,850 to 83,786, and the 17:59 minute traded 144.6 BTC in 607 trades, against 7.9 BTC in the minute before. A volume-weighted or per-trade average leans on that heavy, falling minute and lands below the threshold, which fits the venue's NO. This is a hypothesis: hyperkeeper didn't save the trades, and the API keeps no trade history.
- **10514 can't be inspected.** Its 1-minute candles are already past the API's retention (about 3.5 days), and there were no saved samples.

Both `skew` misses are trade-based markets, missed in opposite directions. 19 of the 243 `skew` outcomes landed within 3 bp of the threshold, and the two misses are among them.

**Change since:** the templates say "TWAP" without saying how samples are weighted. hyperkeeper now saves every recorded window's raw samples (time, price and, for trades, size) from 30 s before the window opens, and logs the plain mean and the volume-weighted average next to each prediction. The next `skew` close calls will show which average the venue uses.

## Method

- **Data:** hyperkeeper's own JSONL logs on a GCP e2-small in Tokyo, one per service start.
- **Lag:** measured from expiry to when hyperkeeper first saw the settlement. It checks every 30 s, so each lag can overstate the true one by up to 30 s.
- **Coverage:** a restart drops the markets whose window is open at that moment, and those aren't counted. Of the markets followed, none went unpredicted and none stayed unsettled.
