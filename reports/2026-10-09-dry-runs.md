# Dry runs, Oct 8–9, 2026

Three hyperkeeper services ran side by side on a GCP e2-small in Tokyo, none able to send anything that takes effect. All three were healthy throughout: no crash restarts. At 06:31 UTC Oct 9, Ubuntu's automatic security updates restarted the HIP-3 shadow and the standby cleanly. That is the kind of brief, planned outage a failover pair covers.

## 1. HIP-4 settlement: 369 of 369 predicted correctly

`hyperkeeper hip4-shadow` followed every mainnet `binaryPrice` market from Oct 8 10:16 to Oct 9 10:40 UTC. It predicted each one at expiry from the price the template names, then checked the venue's settlement.

| Venue | Settles on | Markets | Matched | Venue's settle lag, median / max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `out` | mark price, 60 s TWAP (recorded live) | 298 | **298** | 28 s / 48 s |
| `skew` | BTC trades, 90 s TWAP (recorded live) | 47 | **47** | 53 s / 68 s |
| `txyz` | 1-minute candle closes, 300 s TWAP | 24 | **24** | **35 min / 60 min** |
| **Total** | | **369** | **369** | |

- **No market went unpredicted**: recording started before every live-recorded window opened.
- **Close calls were all right.** 55 predictions landed within 5 bp of their threshold and 12 within 2 bp, the closest at 0.20 bp, and every one settled as predicted. So the recorded mark and trade TWAPs reproduce what the venues compute, not just the obvious cases.
- **`txyz` settled 16 to 60 minutes after expiry**, in two batches: the 13:35 UTC expiries 50–60 minutes later, the 20:00 UTC ones 16–21 minutes later. The other two venues settle within a minute.
- **hyperkeeper had every result about 13 s after expiry** (median 13.2 s, max 14.0 s, for all three venues). A keeper with the settlement grant could settle `txyz`'s markets that soon.
- The lag is measured from expiry to when hyperkeeper first saw the settlement. It checks every 30 s, so each lag can overstate the true one by up to 30 s.

## 2. Failover rehearsal: a backup would have stepped in 34 times

A dry-run standby followed `ignp`'s oracle key (`0xa595…c403`) through the explorer for 24.7 hours (Oct 8 09:58 → Oct 9 10:41 UTC), with a 15 s takeover threshold above that key's normal ~12 s cadence.

- **34 takeovers**, about 1.4 an hour. Each began when the key had been silent 15–20 s (median 17.1 s).
- Each time, the backup would have sent updates until the key resumed: **median 15 s, longest 96 s** (Oct 9 08:56 UTC), **14.2 minutes in total**.
- After the fix that measures silence when the explorer answered, there were no false takeovers while the key was updating normally.

## 3. HIP-3 shadow: still within ~1 bp

Since the 06:31 restart: 5,024 rounds, none unsent, 6 sources per round. The median deviation from `ignp`'s oracle is 0.6–1.8 bp, and `ignp`'s oracle still changes about every 12.3 s, with one gap of 240 s on DOGE.

## What it adds up to

On one test DEX and three mainnet HIP-4 venues, over a day:

- **HIP-3:** the keeper prices within about 1 bp of the deployer's own oracle, updates 4× as often, and as a backup would have covered 34 silences, the longest 96 s.
- **HIP-4:** it computes every settlement the venues computed (369 of 369), about 13 s after expiry, including one venue that took 16 to 60 minutes.
